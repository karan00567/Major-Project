D-04  THE RETRIEVAL GATE: `Recall@60 >= 0.80` is currently unreachable.
      What replaces it?
      Affects: T-212, T-213, `configs/default.yaml`, the evaluation chapter
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the decision TASKLIST names explicitly as "a decision for the human,
not a number to chase."

MEASURED 2026-08-26, after two harness defects were found and fixed:

    run                        pool    union recall   Recall@60 (the gate)
    2026-08-25, unranked        447        0.371              0.029
    max_per_source=25, ranked   493        0.371              0.057
    max_per_source=100, ranked 1937        0.714              0.029

Read the middle column first. Union recall is the fraction of your 35 gold
papers that appear ANYWHERE in the retrieved pool. It is a hard ceiling: no
reranker, however good, can put a paper in the top 60 that was never fetched.

    At max_per_source=25 that ceiling is 0.371.
    At max_per_source=100 it is 0.714.
    The gate is 0.80.

So the gate cannot be met at either setting even with a theoretically perfect
ranker. This is not a tuning problem. There are two independent hurdles:
first the union ceiling must exceed 0.80, and only then must 28 of 35 gold
papers be ranked into 60 slots.

WHY, diagnosed three ways:
  - Of the 10 gold papers never retrieved at mps=100, 10/10 are present in
    OpenAlex, 0 are excluded by the year_min=2019 window, and 9/10 are
    findable by exactly the short-query shape already in use. They were not
    unreachable. They were never asked for.
  - Gold papers that ARE pooled rank 38 times across the 15 sub-question
    rankings, but only 2 reach rank <= 4 - and rank <= 4 is all that 60 papers
    split over 15 sub-questions can take.
  - The stock reranker is above chance but only just: 2 top-4 hits against
    1.19 expected at random. Where a query was specific ("scientific survey
    generation") gold landed at ranks 1 and 2. It is under-informed by generic
    queries, not broken.

The 15 queries used are hand-written stand-ins. Generating good ones is the
Planner's job (T-402), which does not exist yet.

OPTIONS
  (a) Keep the gate, re-run after T-402, accept that it may fail.
      Honest, and the re-run is nearly free now (pool caching was added, so it
      costs a re-rank rather than another 18 minutes of S2 backoff). Risk:
      you find out on ~Aug 31 that it still fails, with no time to react.

  (b) Restate the gate to match the geometry.  [RECOMMENDED]
      Report BOTH numbers and gate on the one that measures retrieval rather
      than corpus-size arithmetic:
        - primary gate: union recall over the retrieved pool >= 0.80
          (this measures "did we find it")
        - secondary, reported not gated: Recall@max_papers
          (this measures "did we keep it", and is bounded by max_papers/35)
      This is defensible in the report because it is exactly what the
      measurement showed, and stating it is stronger than quietly missing a
      number you set before you had data.

  (c) Raise `max_papers` from 60 to ~120 so the top-N has room.
      60 papers over ~15 sub-questions is 4 slots each. Doubling gives 8.
      Cost: every downstream stage doubles - the Extractor is 26.6s/paper, so
      120 papers is ~53 minutes of extraction alone, against section 0.2's
      stated ~30-minute tolerance. It also doubles free-tier request
      consumption, which is a hard quota, not a speed setting.

  (d) Bet entirely on T-402 emitting far more specific queries.
      The diagnose_misses evidence says this is plausible - 9/10 misses were
      findable by a short query. But it is a bet on unwritten code, and it has
      to lift union recall from 0.371 to above 0.80.

  (e) Some combination of (b) and a modest max_papers rise (60 -> 80).

RECOMMENDATION: (b) now, and re-run T-212 immediately after T-402 lands. Decide
(c) only with the T-402 numbers in hand.

ANSWER: b

D-07  T-323: how do you pair a sentence that cites three papers?
      Affects: T-322, T-325, and what the cross-check actually measures
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Only arises if you keep Phase 3B (see D-01). TASKLIST flags it directly: "a
claim sentence citing three papers is not cleanly attributable to one - decide
the rule and write it down, because it changes what the cross-check measures."

OPTIONS
  (a) Drop multi-cite sentences entirely.  [RECOMMENDED]
      Cleanest semantics: every surviving pair is one claim, one source, which
      is exactly what your verifier is trained to judge. Cost: you lose a
      chunk of the data, and survey prose cites multiply quite often, so
      expect to lose a lot. Report the drop rate - it is a limitation, not a
      flaw.
  (b) Pair the sentence against the union of all cited abstracts.
      Keeps the data, but now you are measuring "is this claim supported by
      SOME of this evidence set", which is a different and easier task than
      the one the verifier does in the pipeline. If you choose this, say so
      plainly in the report; a reviewer who notices it unprompted will not be
      generous.
  (c) Pair against each cited abstract separately and score as supported if
      any one supports it.
      Most faithful to how a human wrote the sentence, but it multiplies the
      pair count and biases toward SUPPORT.

Whichever you pick, it goes in the report's method section verbatim.

ANSWER: a
